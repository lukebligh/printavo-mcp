# Q4 KICKOFF — ALL HANDS
**Draft 1 · October 1, 2026 · ~4 minutes spoken · Luke Bligh**

---

## THE SPEECH

**Before anything else — last quarter.**

We set six goals in July. We hit all six. The pricing work, the top accounts, the reorder flows, the line of credit, the supplier list, the handbook. Six for six.

I want to say that out loud because we don't say it enough around here. That was a good quarter. Thank you.

---

**Now the part I've been putting off.**

When Ian left in July, nothing dropped. The receiving got done. The counts got done. The boxes got packed. Not because we planned for it — because you picked it up. For three months. Nobody asked you to and nobody thanked you properly.

That's not a system. That's nine people being decent.

Decent runs out. So I spent the last few weeks writing down what's actually true about this shop — who does what, who owns what, and where I'm in the way.

---

**Here's what changed.**

*[show the chart]*

**Richie owns the week.** The schedule, the art, the screens. What we're making tomorrow is his call, made before he leaves today.

**Justin owns the day.** Once the plan exists, running it is his — the press, the goods, the staging, the quality. When there's a call to make on the floor, he makes it. Not me.

That's the biggest change in this whole thing. Two jobs that used to be one blurry job with my name quietly on both.

---

**Everybody owns a number.**

Not a report. A number. Yours.

Tom and Jerry — what got printed, and your own misprints. Bryan — sews and transfers. Kim — reclaim, catching, and counts out matching counts in. Clint — every customer hearing from us before they have to ask. Richie — screens ready for tomorrow, five days out of five. Justin — on time, and the misprint rate.

You report it. Not me reading it off a sheet.

---

**On staging.**

I already talked to most of you about this. Tom, Jerry, Kim — you said yes before I finished asking. That mattered more than you know.

So here it is plainly: when the press stops, we stage the next day. It's part of the shift, not extra. And when we're busy, that's where the hours are. Justin calls who does what.

---

**Now the honest part.**

Right now I'm sitting in four jobs. Sales. Operations. The money. And whatever else lands that day.

That's the real problem with this business. Not the equipment. Not the people. Not the work. Me being the glue. Every time something has to come through me, it waits.

The plan is to get me out of three of those seats over the next two years, and the first step is this quarter. We've set six goals. I own three. The other three belong to people in this room — on purpose.

---

**Two things you'll notice.**

**November 1, prices go up.** About seven percent direct, a little more on contract. First increase in two years, and the minimum wage in this state went up twenty-two percent in that time. If a customer asks you, that's the honest answer.

**And we're going to meet more, but shorter.** Every Monday the leadership group sits down for ninety minutes. Every morning, the floor meets with Richie. Numbers, problems, done. If a meeting runs long, it's broken and we'll fix it.

---

**Last thing.**

We have one value here, and it's not on a poster. **We give a damn.**

That's what we actually sell. Not shirts — anybody can print a shirt. We sell the fact that we catch it before it ships, we answer before dark, and we remember your specs so you never have to repeat yourself.

Everything I just showed you exists so we can keep doing that at twice this size without it all landing on one person.

That's the quarter. Let's go.

## DELIVERY NOTES

**Length.** About four minutes. Short on purpose. The chart does the explaining; this frames it.

**Don't read it.** Know the six beats and talk: Q3 win → Ian and what you all covered → Richie owns the week, Justin owns the day → everyone owns a number → the staging deal → I'm in four seats and that's the problem.

**The one pause that matters.** After "nine people being decent." Let it sit. That's the line that buys you the rest of the speech.

**Hand out the Accountability Chart PDF** at "here's what changed," not before. If they have it in their hands early they'll read instead of listen.

## THREE THINGS TO DECIDE BEFORE YOU GIVE THIS

**1. Cash — I left it out.**
The balance is $34,589, roughly two weeks of revenue. I did not put that in the speech. In an all-hands, a number like that reads as *should I be looking for a job*, and it would overshadow everything else. It belongs in the Monday leadership meeting, where there are five people who can do something about it.
If you disagree, the place to add it is right after "the honest part" — and if you say it, say what you're doing about it in the same breath.

**2. Clint and the fall list.**
The speech says three of the six goals belong to people in the room. One of those is Clint closing the $105,437 reactivation list — **and you haven't asked him yet.**
Ask him before this meeting. Not during it. Nobody should find out in front of eight colleagues that they own a six-figure number.

**3. Richie and Justin have question marks next to their names.**
The chart shows both of them with "Wants it?" unresolved, because neither has been asked directly whether they want their seat. That's honest, and I'd keep it honest — but have both conversations privately first.
Otherwise Richie learns in a group meeting that his boss isn't sure he wants the job. That's how you lose him.

## WHAT THIS SPEECH DELIBERATELY DOES NOT DO

- **No vision statement, no three-year number.** $3MM by 2028 means nothing to someone catching shirts at a dryer. It belongs in the leadership meeting.
- **No EOS vocabulary.** No "Rocks," no "Level 10," no "Accountability Chart" as a term. They're goals, a Monday meeting, and a chart of who owns what. Teach the words later, if ever.
- **No apology for the price increase.** State it, give the reason, move on.
- **No promises about hours.** It says where the hours *are* when it's busy. It does not promise forty.
